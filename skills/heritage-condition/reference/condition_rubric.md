# Condition Rubric

Supports the `heritage-condition` command, and Phase 3 of `heritage-assess`. Defines four condition levels for
assessing heritage building fabric. Condition is assessed per element and is a
separate axis from significance.

When uncertain between two levels, choose the more severe one.

---

## GOOD

**Headline:** Stable — revisit at the next annual check.

**Definition:** No visible defects, or only very minor surface marks or dust.
No active deterioration. No immediate action needed.

**Examples:**
- Clean plaster, sound paintwork, intact masonry with no visible cracking
- Stable, dry surfaces with no signs of water ingress or staining
- Timber elements intact, painted, no rot, no biological growth
- Windows and doors operating correctly, putty and glazing intact

---

## FAIR

**Headline:** Routine upkeep — repaint, clean or seal as needed.

**Definition:** Minor to moderate defects present but deterioration appears stable
or slow. Routine maintenance by an owner or standard tradesperson (painter, glazier,
handyperson) is likely sufficient — no heritage specialist needed yet.
Review within 12 months.

**Examples:**
- Paintwork weathered, faded or peeling — needs repainting, timber beneath is sound
- Window putty cracked or missing in sections — needs reputtying and repainting
- Superficial surface staining, dirt or soiling on masonry, render or stone
- Hairline cracks (under 1mm) in plaster, render or masonry — stable, not widening
- Slight efflorescence (white salt bloom) on brick or stone surfaces
- Small areas of missing mortar not exposing the brick face directly to weather
- Minor biological growth (moss, lichen) on surfaces — not penetrating joints
- Window or door hardware worn but functional — lubrication or minor adjustment
- Gutters and downpipes with minor debris buildup — needs clearing

---

## POOR

**Headline:** Consult a heritage professional.

**Definition:** Significant defects affecting the building fabric that go beyond
repainting, cleaning or minor sealing. A tradesperson with heritage experience is
needed within 3–6 months. Do not attempt DIY repairs on these items.

**Examples:**
- Timber repair needed — damaged sills, rails or frames (not just paint failure)
- Corroded or failing metal elements (iron lintels, cresting, ties) causing movement
- Widespread paint failure exposing bare timber or metal directly to weather
- Active water ingress, or signs of ongoing damp penetrating the wall fabric
- Large cracks (wider than 2mm) in plaster, render or masonry
- Spalling plaster — sections detached, hollow-sounding or falling
- Biological growth penetrating mortar joints or stone pores
- Missing or damaged roof or gutter sections allowing water entry
- Evidence of structural movement (doors or windows out of square, floors uneven)

---

## CRITICAL

**Headline:** Seek urgent specialist heritage advice.

**Definition:** Severe defects threatening structural integrity, occupant safety,
or irreversible loss of heritage fabric. Seek specialist heritage professional
advice urgently — do not attempt DIY repairs.

**Examples:**
- Major structural cracking, bulging or leaning walls, risk of partial collapse
- Iron or steel structural elements severely corroded and causing masonry to move
- Active subsidence visible in floor levels or wall alignments
- Roof or floor failure creating immediate safety or weather-exposure risk
- Significant biological invasion actively destroying important historic surfaces
- Major water ingress into structural timber (subfloor rot, beam decay)
- Parapet or chimney instability posing a fall risk

---

## How condition feeds Phase 4

Condition sets urgency; significance sets care. The two combine:

| Condition | If significance is High/Exceptional | If significance is Little/Intrusive |
|-----------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Critical  | Urgent specialist intervention; protect fabric. | Urgent for safety only; removal may be an option. |
| Poor      | Heritage-experienced repair, repair over replace. | Repair or remove as practical. |
| Fair      | Routine maintenance, sympathetic materials. | Routine maintenance. |
| Good      | Maintain and monitor. | Maintain or leave. |
